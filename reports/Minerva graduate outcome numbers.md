# ATLAS's college figures check out, but they need labels

Every per-college number the ATLAS app shows today matches, digit for digit, both Minerva's live Majors & Concentrations page and the five "[MARCH 2026] Grads after Graduation" brochures behind it. Those brochures are the newest and most detailed undergraduate outcome data Minerva publishes anywhere, so ATLAS is already showing the right source ([Majors & Concentrations](https://www.minerva.edu/undergraduate/majors-concentrations/)). The problem is what the numbers mean. Each college defines its headline differently: Business and Computational Sciences count internships as a good outcome, Natural Sciences and Social Sciences count only full-time jobs or graduate study, and Arts & Humanities counts "employed in various industries". Three colleges publish breakdowns that do not add up to their own headline. The overall "91% within six months" that Minerva repeats on at least seven web pages is really a single November 2022 snapshot of the classes of 2019-2022, and the source document never mentions a six-month window ([Professional Ventures report, Oct 2023](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/653151df4cc118548b1e8f80_M24_ProfessionalVentures_10192023_Final_WEB.pdf)). No independent body has checked any of Minerva's placement rates. Federal data covers only completion and retention, and Minerva publishes no federal earnings data because it is outside Title IV. The accreditor's 2025-26 special visit did not review placement at all. My recommendation is to keep the March 2026 college figures, but split each headline into full-time work, advanced degrees and internships, show the cohort, sample size and definition next to every number, and drop the Natural Sciences "Graduate Education 50%" figure. The 91% should appear in ATLAS only with its real cohort and date, next to Minerva's own completion rate of 83.76%.

## Minerva publishes outcome data at two levels, and its websites mix them

At the **institution level**, Minerva has only one outcomes dataset: the "Professional Ventures / Alumni Success Report". Its versions are dated August and October 2023, and the October file is still linked from minerva.edu/resources. Its headline block reads "**91% of alumni in full time positions or in graduate programs**", "97% of alumni held an internship role prior to graduation", "15% continuing their education in masters programs, doctorate programs, or academic fellowships", and, in the October version only, "12% of graduates have founded or co-founded their current organization & secured start-up funding". Its entire methodology is one footnote: "Data was collected in November of 2022 for our graduating classes of 2019, 2020, 2021, and 2022. The data reflects 96% of alumni." The report gives no per-college breakdown, no definition of "full time position", and no survey method ([Professional Ventures, Oct 2023](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/653151df4cc118548b1e8f80_M24_ProfessionalVentures_10192023_Final_WEB.pdf); [Aug 2023 version](https://assets.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/64e47abf7262a7914c11178c_M24_ProfessionalVentures_8222023.pdf)).

Minerva's live web pages keep the 91% but change its definition from page to page, and none of them gives a cohort or a sample size. The current outcomes page says "working or in graduate school within six months" ([Student Impact](https://www.minerva.edu/student-impact/)). The undergraduate pages say "employed full-time or in graduate school within 6 months of graduation" ([Undergraduate](https://www.minerva.edu/undergraduate/)). Career Development says "in full-time roles or graduate school", with no time window ([Career Development](https://www.minerva.edu/undergraduate/career-development/)). The Counselors page says "alumni... in full-time positions", also with no window ([Counselors](https://www.minerva.edu/counselors/)). An older page that the parents-of-admits menu still links says "working in meaningful roles" ([Impact & Outcomes (old)](https://www.minerva.edu/impact-outcomes-old/)). Because the source was a point-in-time snapshot of graduates who had left between six months and 3.5 years earlier, the **"within six months" wording is not supported by Minerva's own methodology line**. The companion figures drift in the same way: the internship rate is 97% on most pages but 95% on an older landing page ([Experience Global Immersion](https://www.minerva.edu/experience-global-immersion-at-minerva-university/)), and the count of student ventures is 30, 40+, "more than 100" or "hundreds" depending on the page ([Undergraduate Degrees](https://www.minerva.edu/undergraduatedegrees/); [Parents of Admits](https://www.minerva.edu/parents-of-admits/)).

At the **college level**, the only source is the set of five March 2026 brochures linked from the parents-of-admits page. Their PDF metadata is dated April 10, 2026, and they are image-only. They cover the classes of 2019-2025, except Arts & Humanities, which covers 2019-2023, and each carries a one-line footnote giving its sample size ([AH](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/69e1f7ac9a0c7d8cb75107d3_%5BMARCH%202026%5D%20AH%20Grads%20after%20Graduation.pdf), [BZ](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/69e1f7abc1b69dd02707cc03_%5BMARCH%202026%5D%20BZ%20Grads%20after%20Graduation.pdf), [CS](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/69e1f7ac04cc1509cd5d566b_%5BMARCH%202026%5D%20CS%20Grads%20after%20Graduation.pdf), [NS](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/69e1f7ac0e16e4cfc13618f6_%5BMARCH%202026%5D%20NS%20Grads%20after%20Graduation.pdf), [SS](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/69e1f7acc5e7e58e7c290934_%5BMARCH%202026%5D%20SS%20Grads%20after%20Graduation.pdf)). This is a newer and separate dataset, not a breakdown of the 91%. None of the 27 concentration pages carries an outcome percentage.

Social media adds nothing new. Searches of LinkedIn, Instagram, X, Facebook, YouTube and TikTok found no official 2024-2026 post with an undergraduate outcome figure or any per-college number. The only outlier in an official Minerva channel is a "92%" in founder Ben Nelson's 2020 Medium letter, known only from a search snippet ([Medium, 2020 annual letter](https://medium.com/minerva-university/annual-letter-for-2020-from-our-founder-65a18b95125d)). Social platforms could not be fetched directly during this research, so "none found" reflects what search engines index, not a confirmed absence.

## Five colleges, five definitions, three sums that fail

The college headlines cannot be compared with each other as published.

- **Business:** "94.8% ... are employed full-time or completing internships". The figure only matches the arithmetic if it also includes the one graduate in an advanced degree: (73 + 18 + 1) / 97 = 94.85%.
- **Computational Sciences:** "95.6% ... are actively working or enrolled in advanced degree programs". The 95.6% includes 11.1% "pursued internships".
- **Natural Sciences and Social Sciences:** both count full-time employment or advanced degrees only.
- **Arts & Humanities:** 82% "employed in various industries" plus 18% in advanced degrees, which adds up to exactly 100%. No one is counted as unemployed or unknown.

All figures are from the Majors & Concentrations page and the March 2026 brochures linked above.

Remove internships, and the comparable rate of graduates in **full-time work or an advanced degree** is about **76% for Business, 85% for Computational Sciences, 85% for Natural Sciences and 94% for Social Sciences**. That reorders the colleges: Business drops from second-highest to lowest. This is my arithmetic from Minerva's published parts.

Several published figures contradict each other:

- **Social Sciences:** the breakdown is 74% + 20% + 7% = **101%** against a 93% headline. Even the headline's own two categories sum to 94%. On n = 105 the parts work out to about 106 people, which is more than the sample.
- **Natural Sciences:** the industry chart shows "**Graduate Education 50%**" (about 23 of 46 graduates), but the brochure also says "43.5% are pursuing an advanced degree" (20 of 46). Every NS percentage is an exact fraction of 46, so both use the same base and still disagree by three people.
- **Computational Sciences:** 11.1% and 10.3% do not correspond to whole people out of 256, which suggests a different, unstated base.
- **"Highest rate" claims:** the CS brochure says CS + Business has "the highest rate among all double major combinations" at 79.1% full-time, while the SS brochure gives SS + Business the same title at 76%.
- **Copy errors:** the AH brochure contains text pasted from the SS brochure ("Each of the four concentrations in the Social Science college"). The NS brochure mentions "four concentrations" but lists three rows.

The samples are also smaller than the populations they describe. Each brochure says Minerva "tracked outcomes for more than 300" Business, Computational Sciences and Social Sciences graduates, and "more than 130" Natural Sciences graduates, yet the headline samples are 97, 256, 105 and 46. Only Social Sciences states its selection rule: "surveyed graduates with Social Sciences as their sole major". No brochure gives a response rate or explains how a "within six months" status was measured for classes that graduated in 2019-2021. For comparison, federal completions data for 2024-25 lists 13 Biology, 28 Business, 76 Computer and Information Sciences, 25 Social Sciences and 20 Liberal Arts bachelor's first majors, 162 in total ([NCES College Navigator](https://nces.ed.gov/collegenavigator/?id=484844)). That is consistent with Computational Sciences dominating the dataset and with Natural Sciences being thin: 46 graduates spread over seven classes.

## Independent sources cover completion, not careers

**No independent source measures Minerva's placement rates, overall or by college.** Every journalist's figure traces back to Minerva. Forbes reported 88% "within six months of graduation" in 2021 ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/nelldebevoise/2021/07/29/minerva-universitys-plan-to-out-elite-harvard/)). The 2024 *Daedalus* essay ("15 percent... graduate and professional schools; another 15 percent started their own ventures") was written by Minerva's founding president and its chief academic officer ([Daedalus](https://www.amacad.org/sites/default/files/publication/downloads/Daedalus_Sp24_22_Cannon%20&%20Kosslyn.pdf)); its exact wording could not be checked against the PDF. Niche's "91% employed 1 year after graduation" matches Minerva's own headline exactly, and its source is unstated ([Niche](https://www.niche.com/colleges/minerva-university/after-college/)). Minerva does not take part in federal Title IV aid, so College Scorecard has no earnings record to check employment quality against ([WSCUC Special Visit Team Report](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/69b426e1703eed3ca118d714_MU%20Special%20Visit%20(SV)%20Team%20Report%20FINAL%20Draft%202026.01.02%20(1).pdf), p. 3).

The accreditor did not audit Minerva's career outcomes either. The December 2025 WSCUC special visit covered finances, communication, cost of attendance, library resources and natural-science labs. The only outcome numbers in the visit report are figures Minerva supplied and the team relays:

- "98% of Natural Sciences students whose stated goal was to pursue graduate school obtained research experiences in external research laboratories"
- "over 50% of the 2019 to 2024 Natural Sciences cohorts undertook a laboratory science research-related internship"

The team concluded that "Minerva graduates have been successful thus far", but it asked Minerva to understand "how well the internship program serves students in the aggregate", in addition to its "deep and commendable understanding of the success of individual students" (Team Report, pp. 26-28, linked above). The Commission's February 2026 action letter does not mention graduation rates or career outcomes at all ([WSCUC Action Letter](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/69b426e177b6b85d9e85edf1_CAL_260227_MINERVA_SV.pdf)).

On completion, independent and Minerva figures broadly agree, but they disagree on definitions:

- **Minerva's own figures:** its November 2025 PDF gives 150%-time graduation rates of 75.00% (founding class) to 90.18% (class of 2023), with **83.76% across all cohorts** and 85.13% for the last three. First-to-second-year retention is **94.62% across all cohorts** ([Completion and Retention Rates, Nov 2025](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/6915f03bace3ce80e1ea0cfe_Completion%20and%20Retention%20Rates%20-%20November%202025.pdf)).
- **Federal data:** College Navigator shows **98% retention** for students who entered in fall 2023 and **89% bachelor's completion after eight years** for students who entered in 2016-17. The 89% covers only students who did not receive Pell Grants, and Navigator shows no standard graduation rate ([NCES College Navigator](https://nces.ed.gov/collegenavigator/?id=484844)).
- **Aggregators:** sites such as DataUSA quote 88.8%, which could not be traced to an ed.gov page.
- **Other Minerva statements:** Minerva's own U.S. News announcement gives a "four-year graduation rate" of 79% ([U.S. News announcement](https://www.minerva.edu/announcements/minerva-university-ranks-no-1-in-three-categories-in-2027-u-s-news-best-colleges-rankings/)). The WSCUC team describes retention as "historically between 95 and 97 percent", which is higher than Minerva's own all-cohort 94.62%.

None of these numbers contradicts the placement claims. They do qualify them: "91% of graduates" leaves out the roughly one in six entrants who never graduate.

Compared with what ATLAS shows today, the changes are:

- **Labels:** replace each college's single headline percentage with the component bars, and label every bar with Minerva's wording.
- **Natural Sciences:** remove the "Graduate Education 50%" industry figure.
- **Arts & Humanities:** show its 2019-2023 window as clearly as the 2019-2025 windows elsewhere.
- **Notes:** add one sentence per college stating its definition and sample.

Applicants should also be told three things the brochures do not say:

1. The samples cover between a third and most of each college's graduates, and the selection method is unknown for four of the five colleges.
2. Double majors may be counted in more than one college: the samples sum to 608, against roughly 1,000 alumni across all programs.
3. No outside party has verified any of these placement figures.

## Conclusion

Minerva is not short of outcome numbers. What it lacks is a single definition and any documented method. Its strongest-looking figures, the Computational Sciences and Business headlines, are high partly because they count internships. Its most-repeated figure, the 91%, has been rewritten into a six-month claim that its own source does not make. The honest picture is still a good one: about 76-94% of surveyed graduates in each college are in full-time work or graduate study, and roughly 84% of entrants graduate. That picture holds up only if ATLAS shows the components and the caveats rather than the headlines.

Doing that also fits the app's purpose. An applicant who sees that Natural Sciences sends 43.5% of graduates to graduate school while Business sends 1%, or that a figure rests on 46 people, learns something real about the choice in front of them, which a single headline percentage cannot teach. The two things that would most improve these numbers are both Minerva's to provide: a methods note (response rate, collection date, double-major rule) and an updated institution-wide report covering the classes of 2023-2026. Until they exist, ATLAS should state plainly that no one outside Minerva has checked these figures.
