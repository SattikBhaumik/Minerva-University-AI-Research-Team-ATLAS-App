# Pathway, degree-planning and career-exploration tools, and the evidence on what helps students choose

Scope note: research done 2026-11-22 with web search. Most findings below come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not full-text reads; treat exact figures as "verify against the original" unless marked otherwise. Evidence-strength labels used throughout: **Strong** (randomized trial or large quasi-experiment, peer-reviewed), **Moderate** (quasi-experimental, matched comparison, or several consistent studies), **Weak** (single-site, self-reported, developer- or vendor-reported, press coverage), **Descriptive** (documents what a tool does; says nothing about impact).

## 1. Which planning and exploration tools are well regarded, and what features distinguish them?

### Takeaway
The well-regarded tools cluster into four feature families: (a) **historical course data** (Carta, Berkeleytime: grade distributions, workload, enrollment history), (b) **term-by-term planners with "what-if" audits** (Stellic and similar degree-audit planners), (c) **schedule builders** (MIT Hydrant, Coursicle), and (d) **career exploration through people and interests** (Roadtrip Nation interviews, O*NET/My Next Move RIASEC profiler, Designing Your Life "Odyssey Plans"). The only tool with a rigorous impact study of a specific feature (Carta) found that showing grade distributions *lowered* GPA and did not change course choice, a warning that more data is not automatically better.

### Cited Findings

**Stanford Carta (historical course data)**
- Launched August 2016; aggregates recent student evaluations and 15 years of registrar records, including each course's workload and grade distribution; more than 90% of Stanford undergraduates had used it. (Descriptive) — [Stanford Magazine](https://stanfordmag.org/contents/choosing-classes-with-a-little-help-from-big-data); [Stanford News 2018](https://news.stanford.edu/2018/05/10/knowing-past-course-data-affects-college-students-gpa/)
- A 2018 Stanford study found that "platform usage caused a significant reduction in GPA during the subsequent academic term"; the harm came from the historical grade data, while workload information did not harm GPA. One proposed (unconfirmed) mechanism: high grade distributions lull students into complacency. (Moderate: quasi-experimental, single institution) — [Stanford News](https://news.stanford.edu/2018/05/10/knowing-past-course-data-affects-college-students-gpa/); [Stanford Daily](https://stanforddaily.com/2018/06/08/carta-use-leads-to-lower-grades-stanford-report-finds/)
- The same work found Carta had no measurable influence on which courses students enrolled in. — [Stanford Daily](https://stanforddaily.com/2018/06/08/carta-use-leads-to-lower-grades-stanford-report-finds/)
- A later paper used over 1 million timestamped course views and 3,336 transcripts (67,000+ enrollments) to study exploration behavior. — [Psychology Today-hosted PDF](https://cdn2.psychologytoday.com/assets/2332858421991148.pdf)
- Stanford's administration requested the grade-distribution metrics be taken down; they were available until March 2020 (status since not verified). — [Stanford Daily 2021](https://stanforddaily.com/2021/04/15/trends-in-stanford-stem-department-grade-distributions/)

**Berkeleytime (UC Berkeley, student-run)**
- Lets users view and compare grade distributions per course and semester, and track real-time enrollment trends and history; it is an official organization under the ASUC Office of the CTO. (Descriptive) — [berkeleytime.com](https://berkeleytime.com)
- No impact evaluation found.

**MIT Hydrant (and Firehose, its predecessor)**
- Student-built, open-source (GPLv3) semester course planner run by SIPB, MIT's volunteer student computing group, updated each semester; used by undergraduates and graduate students to browse classes and build schedules. (Descriptive) — [Hydrant GitHub](https://github.com/sipb/hydrant); [Hydrant wiki](https://github.com/sipb/hydrant/wiki); [MIT Admissions blog](https://mitadmissions.org/blogs/entry/hydranting-my-spring-semester/)
- Search results did not confirm Hydrant's specific features (conflict detection, calendar export); a gap.

**Coursicle**
- Free schedule maker; unlimited schedules, seat-open notifications, Google Calendar sync, export/print. Notifications require account/contact info. (Descriptive) — [Coursicle schedule maker](https://www.coursicle.com/college-schedule-maker/)

**Stellic (and degree-audit planners generally)**
- Drag-and-drop courses from official audit requirements into a term-by-term layout; "what-if" audits show how switching a major, adding a minor, or changing catalog year affects progress. — [University of Delaware Stellic resources](https://www.udel.edu/content/udel/en/students/dem/stellic/student-resources); [Stellic Pathway Planner](https://www.stellic.com/products/pathway-planner)
- Shows when a course is usually offered and whether prerequisites are met while planning. — [Johns Hopkins Stellic](https://info.stellic.jhu.edu)
- Two progress views: "official" (taken/registered) and "planned" (includes future planned courses). — [Stellic Zendesk](https://stellic.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4402412275341)
- Plans are explicitly non-binding ("not a guarantee of course offerings"); a what-if plan is unofficial until the student files a declaration. — [UW-La Crosse](https://www.uwlax.edu/records/student-resources/stellic/); [Delaware](https://www.udel.edu/content/udel/en/students/dem/stellic/student-resources)
- Advisors can see plan changes, add notes, and students can submit a plan for review. (Requires institutional accounts.) — [Case Western](https://case.edu/registrar/registration-classes/degree-planning)
- No independent impact study of Stellic found.

**Guided Pathways program maps (CCRC model)**
- Programs grouped into broad career-focused "meta-majors" with program maps showing course sequences; colleges use other labels ("academic and career communities," "pre-majors"). Example: San Jacinto used eight meta-majors; Tri-C nine. (Descriptive) — [CCRC case study: San Jac](https://ccrc.tc.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/guided-pathways-case-study-3-san-jac.pdf); [CCRC case study: Tri-C](https://ccrc.tc.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/guided-pathways-case-study-1-tri-c.pdf)
- CCRC warns that many colleges focus on the structural pieces (maps) "rather than using program maps to reshape the student experience." — [CCRC Guided Pathways Part 1](https://ccrc.tc.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/guided-pathways-part-1-theory-practice.pdf)
- CCRC has a public "CC Program Mapper" guide (2026). — [CCRC guide to the CC Program Mapper](https://ccrc.tc.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/guide-to-the-cc-program-mapper.pdf)

**Roadtrip Nation (people-centered career exploration)**
- The Roadtrip Nation Experience is a 12-lesson high school career exploration program that culminates in the student interviewing a person about how they got where they are. (Descriptive) — [Roadtrip Nation impact](https://roadtripnation.com/pages/impact)
- EPIC third-party evaluation (2011-12, three San Jose PLUS academies, Hewlett Deeper Learning Rubric): participants' GPAs rose 2.35 to 2.75 vs 2.6 for others; positive effects on self-efficacy, problem solving, critical thinking. (Weak: single district, pre-2015, small) — [EdSource 2015](https://edsource.org/2015/putting-alternative-school-students-on-the-road-to-career/76206)
- Self-reported: 69% said the course helped them see education as relevant to their life; 24% increase in ability to seek out role models and network. (Weak: organization's own surveys, no comparison groups stated) — [Roadtrip Nation impact](https://roadtripnation.com/pages/impact)

**O*NET Interest Profiler / My Next Move**
- Free RIASEC (Holland) interest inventory for educational planning and career exploration; 60-item Short Form, 30-item Mini-IP, long form; delivered on the web through My Next Move; the manual documents reliability and validity. (Descriptive / psychometric) — [O*NET IP Manual](https://onetcenter.org/reports/IP_Manual.html); [IP Short Form psychometrics](https://www.onetcenter.org:443/dl_files/IPSF_Psychometric.pdf)
- A 2022 *Journal of Career Assessment* comparison of four RIASEC inventories found scores highly correlated but the instruments often gave the same person **different high-point codes**, so RIASEC results are not interchangeable. (Moderate) — [Illinois Experts: "What Do Interest Inventories Measure?"](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/what-do-interest-inventories-measure-the-convergence-and-content-/); [CSULB summary](https://www.csulb.edu/college-of-business/cob-accreditation-aacsb/article/what-do-interest-inventories-measure)

**College Scorecard (outcome statistics)**
- Hurwitz & Smith (Economic Inquiry 2018) used all SAT score sends around the Sept 2015 Scorecard release: newly released median-earnings data shifted score sending (score sends from private-high-school students rose 4.1% per 10% higher median earnings), but already-available cost and graduation-rate data did not. (Strong: universe of score sends, natural experiment) — [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/is-college-choice-impacted-by-data-in-the-college-scorecard); [EconPapers abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:ecinqu:v:56:y:2018:i:2:p:1220-1243)
- Responsiveness was stronger among Asian and White students than Black and Hispanic students; the biggest responders were those who already had the most resources and information; little evidence enrollment counts changed. — [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/blog/brown-center-chalkboard/2016/04/29/is-college-choice-impacted-by-data-in-the-college-scorecard/); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:ecinqu:v:56:y:2018:i:2:p:1220-1243)

**Designing Your Life (Stanford design-thinking course; "Odyssey Plans")**
- Odyssey Plans: ideate three alternative five-year plans; the method reframes "find the one best plan" as "I have the capacity for multiple great lives and plans." — [Colorado State Odyssey Plans](https://bizcareers.colostate.edu/resources/exploring-career-options-odyssey-plans/); [European Proceedings 2019](https://europeanproceedings.com/article/10.15405/epsbs.2019.11.19)
- A 2019 evaluation of a "Design your Career" programme measured dysfunctional career beliefs, self-efficacy, affect and career uncertainty; treatment group showed fewer dysfunctional career beliefs than control. (Weak: small, single-site; the search summary suggests the gap existed at baseline, so the causal claim is unclear) — [European Proceedings 2019](https://europeanproceedings.com/article/10.15405/epsbs.2019.11.19)

**Degree Compass (Austin Peay; course recommender)**
- Netflix-style course recommender; developer claimed 90% accuracy predicting C-or-better; six-year graduation rose 33% to 37.4% after 2011 introduction; students reportedly did half a letter grade better in recommended courses. (Weak: developer/funder/press-reported; no independent or randomized evaluation found) — [EDUCAUSE Review](https://er.educause.edu/articles/2012/9/austin-peay-state-university-degree-compass); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/alltechconsidered/2013/12/18/255298799/because-you-liked-chemistry-we-recommend-these-classes); [EdSurge 2017, "deeper questions emerged"](https://edsurge.com/news/2017-04-13-this-mathematician-brought-big-data-to-advising-then-deeper-questions-emerged)

### Inferences
- The features that distinguish the best student-built tools (Carta, Berkeleytime, Hydrant) are speed, a single searchable surface, and *historical* data. ATLAS's structured content (pathways, prerequisite map, rules tracker) already covers the degree-audit family; its distinctive gap is the "people and possibilities" family (Roadtrip-style interviews, Odyssey-style multiple futures).
- The Carta result is directly relevant to ATLAS's outcome-statistics feature: raw numbers that look like "easy/hard" or "pays well/poorly" can change effort or narrow choice without improving decisions. Workload information was the benign part.
- Stellic's "official vs planned" split and "usually offered in" labels are cheap, account-free ideas ATLAS can copy because they are display rules over data ATLAS already has.
- Scorecard evidence shows outcome data moves the already-advantaged most; any ATLAS outcome display should be paired with interpretation that does not assume insider knowledge.

### Gaps
- No primary source fetched for BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, BigFuture, Khan Academy/Coursera career paths, or "What can I do with this major" pages; their feature lists are not documented here and none of them has an impact study I found.
- Hydrant/Firehose feature specifics (conflict detection, export) were not confirmed.
- No independent impact evaluations found for Berkeleytime, Hydrant, Coursicle, Stellic, uAchieve, or My Next Move.
- Carta's exact effect size (in SD units) not retrieved.

## 2. What does research say helps students choose and stay in majors?

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence favors (1) **getting into a program early with a full plan** (CCRC program-entry research), (2) **brief encounters with relatable people from the field** (role-model RCTs with large effects for women), (3) **students writing their own connection between coursework and their goals** (utility-value interventions), (4) **structured career courses that include values clarification** (meta-analyses, d about .30-.60), and (5) **accurate information that corrects misbeliefs** (earnings-information experiments). Low-touch nudges at scale reliably fail. Structural reforms (meta-majors, maps) show only modest, mixed effects when not tied to advising.

### Cited Findings

**Early program entry and full-program plans (CCRC)**
- Students who enter a program of study (e.g., 9+ college-level credits in one area) in their first year complete or transfer at roughly 2-3 times the rate of later entrants. (Moderate: large administrative datasets, observational) — [CCRC "Get With the Program"](https://ccrc.tc.columbia.edu/publications/get-with-the-program.html); [Laney summary](https://laney.edu/midterm-report-appendices/wp-content/uploads/sites/250/2012/03/Ideas-from-and-triggered-by-Davis-Jenkins-CCRC-paper-v4-4-25-11.pdf)
- Washington State follow-up (62,000+ students, 7 years): about a third of second-year entrants completed or transferred; about 20% of third-year entrants. (Moderate, observational; selection effects likely) — [CCRC "Get With the Program... and Finish It"](https://ccrc.tc.columbia.edu/publications/get-with-the-program-finish-it.html)
- CCRC's Ask-Connect-Inspire-Plan onboarding model (from ~120 colleges): ongoing conversations about interests, strengths and circumstances (Ask); at least one well-taught first-term course on a topic the student finds interesting (Inspire); a full-program educational plan by the end of the first term (Plan). Colleges often manage Ask/Connect/Inspire but "have trouble getting to Plan." (Descriptive/practice-based) — [CCRC ACIP](https://ccrc.tc.columbia.edu/publications/ask-connect-inspire-plan-onboarding.html); [CCRC blog](https://ccrc.tc.columbia.edu/easyblog/acip-reimagining-onboarding.html); [CCCCO brief](https://www.cccco.edu:443/-/media/CCCCO-Website/docs/gp-playbook/CAGP-Institute-Insights-Connecting-Students-to-Programs-Brief.pdf)

**Guided Pathways / meta-majors at scale (mixed)**
- Two large studies (AACC Pathways, NSF): first-year credit accumulation rose, but persistence declined at both sets of colleges (partly COVID); no evidence that adopting 5+ practices improved early momentum; no improvement in college-level course success. (Moderate, mixed/null) — [CCRC blog: two large studies](https://ccrc.tc.columbia.edu/easyblog/two-large-studies-measure-progress-guided-pathways.html); [AACC/CCRC findings](https://www.mtsac.edu/president/cabinet-notes/2018-19/AACC_Pathways_CCRC_Research_Findings_041019_Highlighted_Pages.pdf)
- Washington State Institute for Public Policy preliminary report: outcome differences small; cannot attribute to Guided Pathways. (Moderate, null) — [WSIPP](https://wsipp.wa.gov/ReportFile/1789/Wsipp_Guided-Pathways-Preliminary-Report-on-Implementation-and-Student-Outcomes_Report.pdf)

**Role models and alumni information**
- Porter & Serra (AEJ: Applied 2020): two brief (15-minute discussion) classroom visits by charismatic alumnae economics majors raised women's likelihood of majoring in economics by 8 percentage points from a 9% baseline; no effect on men. (Strong: randomized field experiment) — [AEA journal page](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/app.20180426); [AEA research chart](https://www.aeaweb.org/research/charts/gender-role-model-economics)
- Breda, Grenet, Monnet & Van Effenterre (Economic Journal 2023): one-hour visits by 56 women in science to ~20,000 Paris-region high schoolers raised Grade 12 girls' enrollment in selective male-dominated STEM programs from 11% to 14.5%; concentrated among high-achieving girls in math; most effective visits improved perceptions of STEM careers **without overemphasizing women's underrepresentation**; visits also reduced stereotypes. (Strong: RCT) — [PSE publication](https://www.parisschoolofeconomics.eu/en/publications-hal/how-effective-are-female-role-models-in-steering-girls-towards-stem-evidence-from-french-high-schools-2); [IZA DP 13163](https://docs.iza.org/dp13163.pdf)

**Correcting beliefs with accurate information**
- Wiswall & Zafar (NYU undergraduates): students were substantially misinformed about population earnings by major; they updated beliefs after receiving true distributions, and intended major tracked beliefs about own earnings and ability; but heterogeneous tastes were the dominant factor in major choice; positive average welfare gains from the information. (Strong: within-subject information experiment, highly cited) — [NY Fed Staff Report 500](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr500.html)
- Baker, Bettinger, Jacob & Marinescu (Economics of Education Review 2018): California community college students rarely ranked major categories correctly by labor outcomes (under 15% in the published version; under 40% in the 2017 working paper), overestimated salaries ~13% and underestimated employment probability by almost 25%; beliefs about **course enjoyment and grades were the main determinants** of choice; a 1% increase in expected salary associated with a 1.4-1.8% higher probability of choosing that major category. (Strong: experimental) — [NBER w23333](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23333); [UMich EPI](https://edpolicy.umich.edu/research/epi-working-papers/effect-labor-market-information-community-college-students-major-choice)

**Students writing their own relevance (utility value)**
- Utility-value writing (students write how course material connects to their lives/goals) in introductory biology: students were 14 percentage points more likely to be on a STEM-degree path 2.5 years later (69% vs 55%). (Strong: randomized, peer-reviewed; mostly one research group) — [UW-Madison Psychology](https://psych.wisc.edu/news/students-who-see-science-in-the-real-world-are-more-likely-to-stick-with-stem-majors/); [PMC6168083](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6168083)
- In an intro-biology study of ~1,040 students the writing process was particularly powerful for first-generation underrepresented students. — [PMC4853302](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4853302)
- Works best for students who doubt their competence or have a history of poor performance; some studies report a puzzling negative interaction with expectancies for success. — [PMC6884362](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6884362)

**Career interventions and career courses**
- Across meta-analyses, career interventions vs no treatment show overall effect sizes of about .30-.60; Whiston et al. (2017) found career classes and individual counseling most effective; computer-assisted interventions **without counselor contact** were less effective than other modalities; critical elements were counselor support, **values clarification**, and psycho-educational components. (Strong: meta-analytic; secondhand summaries) — [Sage Encyclopedia: career intervention outcomes](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/careerdevelopment/chpt/career-intervention-outcomes); [Frontiers 2024 review citing Whiston 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1461503/full); [EconBiz record, Whiston et al. 2017](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/effectiveness-of-career-choice-interventions-a-meta-analytic-replication-and-extension/10011720945)
- Career interventions' largest effects in university students are on career decision-making self-efficacy. (Strong: meta-analysis) — [Ulas meta-analytic review](https://researchgate.net/profile/Oezlem-Ulas/publication/335497508_The_effects_of_career_interventions_on_university_students%27_levels_of_career_decision-making_self-efficacy_A_meta-analytic_review/links/63d40115c465a873a2629b5d/The-effects-of-career-interventions-on-university-students-levels-of-career-decision-making-self-efficacy-A-meta-analytic-review.pdf)
- Florida State's career course (cognitive information processing): matched-cohort study found higher graduation rates for participants despite lower GPA/SAT (Reardon et al. 2015, J. College Student Retention 17(3)); review of career-course studies: 32 of 36 (88%) reported positive gains. (Moderate: quasi-experimental, mostly one institution, technical reports) — [FSU Technical Report 59](https://career.fsu.edu/sites/g/files/upcbnu746/files/TR-59.pdf); [FSU TR 44](https://career.fsu.edu/sites/default/files/CIP/TR%2044.pdf); [ACA Manifold](https://manifold.counseling.org/read/career-course-impact-on-college-students-career-decision-and-affective-states)

**Self-authorship and reflection**
- NACADA study of 132 student narratives: advising sessions focused explicitly on goal reflection and volitional planning enhanced decision-making and self-authoring. (Weak-Moderate: qualitative) — [NACADA Journal](https://nacada.kglmeridian.com/view/journals/jnaa/26/1/article-p32.xml)
- Creamer & Laughlin (2005, 40 women) linked self-authorship to career decision-making; Baxter Magolda turned her longitudinal interview into a reflective conversation guide. Literature is thin and mostly from one research program. (Weak; pre-2015) — [NACADA PDF](https://nacada.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/jnaa/26/1/article-p32.pdf); [Baxter Magolda, Toward Reflective Conversations](https://new.sewanee.edu/files/resources/baxter-magolda-toward-reflective-conversations.pdf)

**Choice overload in course selection**
- Iyengar & Lepper (2000): people more likely to choose from a limited set than an extensive one (consumer settings). (Strong in original domain; replication debated) — [Iyengar & Lepper PDF](https://faculty.washington.edu/jdb/345/345%20Articles/Iyengar%20&%20Lepper%20(2000).pdf)
- In course selection specifically: an Illinois thesis found thematic vs taxonomic catalog categorization did **not** change choice-overload measures. (Weak) — [IDEALS](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/49405)
- Novices are more likely to make poor choices and feel regret; they benefit from minimal choice; Hechinger reports colleges (e.g., FIU) improving on-time graduation after limiting choices. (Weak: news features) — [Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/some-colleges-start-to-confront-a-surprising-reason-students-fail-too-many-choices/); [QS Insights](https://magazine.qs.com/qs-insights-magazine-43/the-freedom-of-choice-and-the-stress-that-follows)
- HEFCE (UK) learned that more data can lead to poorer student choices. (Weak) — [Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com:443/news/more-data-can-lead-to-poor-student-choices-hefce-learns/2012410.article)

**Nudges**
- Large-scale FAFSA nudge trials (~800,000 students): no detectable effect on enrollment or aid across content, timing, design, and personal-help offers; only 11.6% replied to advising offers. Oreopoulos's 2020 review: many large-scale low-touch nudges find precise null effects. (Strong) — [NBER w26158](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26158.pdf); [IZA DP 13718](https://repec.iza.org/dp13718.pdf); [FutureEd](https://www.future-ed.org/what-happens-when-nudges-dont-work)
- Online/text coaching for enrolled students: precise null effects on credits, grades, retention, though gains in satisfaction and belonging. (Strong) — [NBER w25036](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25036); [Oreopoulos et al. JEBO 2020](https://oreopoulos.faculty.economics.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/oreopoulos-et-al-improving-nonacademic-student-outcomes-using-online-and-text-message-coaching-jebo-2020.pdf)
- Exception: Georgia State's "Pounce" chatbot (RCT, >7,000 admits; Page & Gehlbach, AERA Open) raised on-time enrollment by 3.3 points (21% less summer melt) by handling concrete, local pre-enrollment tasks. (Strong) — [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/freezing-summer-melt-in-its-tracks-increasing-college-enrollment-with-ai/); [Campus Technology](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2017/03/07/using-ai-chatbots-to-freeze-summer-melt-in-higher-ed.aspx)

### Inferences
- Across the best evidence, the effective ingredient is that **students engage their own reasons** (values clarification, writing relevance, interviewing someone) or **meet a real person**; passive delivery of information or reminders has small or null effects.
- Information works when it corrects a specific misbelief the student holds (Wiswall & Zafar; Baker et al.). A "guess first, then reveal" design operationalizes exactly that mechanism.
- Enjoyment and expected grades dominate major choice (Baker et al.), so ATLAS's course descriptions and student stories (what a course feels like) matter at least as much as outcome statistics.
- The role-model RCTs say framing matters: show what the work is like, not only who is missing.
- Meta-major maps alone are not enough; CCRC finds "Plan" is where colleges stall. ATLAS's full multi-year pathway is already the "Plan" piece; what is missing is linking it to the student's stated reasons.
- Pounce shows a chatbot can work for logistics; this is not evidence that conversational AI helps with values-laden choice.

### Gaps
- No Minerva-specific evidence (Minerva's cornerstone first year, global rotation, and late major declaration differ from the US community-college populations behind most CCRC findings).
- Whiston 2017's exact effect sizes for choice outcomes not retrieved (only secondhand summaries).
- No rigorous evidence found on "structured side-by-side comparison" of majors or plans as an intervention by itself.
- No study found on values-based decision tools (e.g., weighting criteria) in major choice specifically; values clarification evidence comes via career-intervention meta-analyses.

## 3. Which patterns support learning and agency versus passivity? (Flagging accounts and AI-generated text)

### Takeaway
Agency-supporting patterns make the student produce something (a guess, a reason, three alternative plans, an interview question, a ranked list of values) and then confront it with real data or a real person. Passivity patterns hand the student an answer (recommendation engines, "you should take X," chatbots, risk scores). Several well-known features (advisor review, seat alerts, cross-device sync, peer reviews, chatbots, Netflix-style recommenders) require accounts or generated text, which ATLAS forbids.

### Cited Findings
- Computer-assisted career interventions without counselor support were less effective than counselor-involved formats; counselor support and values clarification were critical elements. — [Sage Encyclopedia](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/careerdevelopment/chpt/career-intervention-outcomes); [Frontiers 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1461503/full)
- Carta's grade data reduced GPA without changing course choice; workload data did not harm. — [Stanford News](https://news.stanford.edu/2018/05/10/knowing-past-course-data-affects-college-students-gpa/)
- Degree Compass (recommender) evidence is developer-reported; EdSurge reported "deeper questions emerged" for its creator about steering students. — [EdSurge 2017](https://edsurge.com/news/2017-04-13-this-mathematician-brought-big-data-to-advising-then-deeper-questions-emerged)
- Interest inventories give different top codes for the same person, so a single RIASEC result should not be presented as the answer. — [Illinois Experts](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/what-do-interest-inventories-measure-the-convergence-and-content-/)
- Utility-value writing (student-generated relevance) improved persistence; the student, not the tool, writes the connection. — [UW-Madison](https://psych.wisc.edu/news/students-who-see-science-in-the-real-world-are-more-likely-to-stick-with-stem-majors/)
- Roadtrip Nation's core activity is the student conducting an interview. — [Roadtrip Nation impact](https://roadtripnation.com/pages/impact)
- Odyssey Plans have the student draft three different futures. — [Colorado State](https://bizcareers.colostate.edu/resources/exploring-career-options-odyssey-plans/)
- Scorecard data were used most by already-advantaged students. — [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/blog/brown-center-chalkboard/2016/04/29/is-college-choice-impacted-by-data-in-the-college-scorecard/)

Pattern classification (the classification is my analysis; evidence per row is cited above):

### Inferences
- ATLAS's constraints line up with the evidence: the strongest account-free, AI-free patterns are exactly the reflective ones. Advisor-in-the-loop can be approximated without accounts by an exportable/printable plan the student brings to a human (file or URL hash), keeping the human conversation the evidence says matters.
- Outcome statistics should never be ranked or scored into a "best major"; show distributions and invite the student to compare against their own guess and values.

### Gaps
- No study found that directly compares reflective vs recommender interfaces on decision quality in degree planning.
- No evidence found on privacy-first or local-only planning tools as a category.

## 4. Concrete small-to-medium feature ideas for ATLAS, each tied to evidence

### Takeaway
Ten account-free, AI-free features follow from the evidence, the strongest being guess-then-reveal on outcomes, a "my reasons" note attached to saved plans, an Odyssey-style three-futures comparison, a values-weighted plan comparison, and an interview kit paired with student stories. Each makes the student do the thinking and keeps all data in the browser.

### Cited Findings (evidence each idea rests on)
1. **Guess-then-reveal outcome cards.** Before seeing a major's outcome statistic, the student enters a guess; ATLAS then shows the real figure and the gap. Rests on: students are substantially misinformed and update when shown true data — [NY Fed SR500](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr500.html); [NBER w23333](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23333). Strength: Strong. Local storage only; no AI.
2. **"Why this?" note on each planned course and on each saved plan** (one-line prompt: "How does this connect to something you care about?"), shown back in plan comparison. Rests on: utility-value writing raised STEM persistence 14 points — [UW-Madison](https://psych.wisc.edu/news/students-who-see-science-in-the-real-world-are-more-likely-to-stick-with-stem-majors/); reflection-focused advising improved decision-making — [NACADA](https://nacada.kglmeridian.com/view/journals/jnaa/26/1/article-p32.xml). Strength: Strong (UV) / Weak (self-authorship). No AI; student writes.
3. **Three Futures (Odyssey) view.** Prompt the student to save three deliberately different plans (e.g., the obvious one, the alternative, the wild card) and compare them side-by-side with their own notes. Extends existing plan comparison. Rests on: Designing Your Life Odyssey Plans and reduced dysfunctional beliefs — [European Proceedings](https://europeanproceedings.com/article/10.15405/epsbs.2019.11.19); [Colorado State](https://bizcareers.colostate.edu/resources/exploring-career-options-odyssey-plans/). Strength: Weak. No AI.
4. **Values-weighted comparison.** Student picks and orders 3-5 of their own criteria (e.g., breadth, depth in one field, flexibility to switch, fit with rotation city) and scores each plan themselves; ATLAS shows their ratings, never a computed "winner." Rests on: values clarification as a critical element of effective career interventions — [Frontiers 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1461503/full). Strength: Strong-indirect.
5. **Interview kit on student stories.** Next to each human-written story, a short set of human-written questions to ask a real student or alum, and a place to save the answers. Rests on: Roadtrip Nation interview model — [Roadtrip Nation](https://roadtripnation.com/pages/impact); brief alumni contact changing major choice — [AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/app.20180426). Strength: Strong for the role-model ingredient; Weak for the kit format.
6. **Stories framed around the work, not the gap.** Editorial rule for student stories: describe what the person does day to day and how the major helped. Rests on: Breda et al., most effective visits improved perceptions of careers without overemphasizing underrepresentation — [PSE](https://www.parisschoolofeconomics.eu/en/publications-hal/how-effective-are-female-role-models-in-steering-girls-towards-stem-evidence-from-french-high-schools-2). Strength: Strong. Content rule, no code.
7. **"Official vs planned" and "usually offered in" labels** on the course tracker and pathway. Rests on: Stellic's two progress views and offering-term display — [Stellic Zendesk](https://stellic.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4402412275341); [JHU Stellic](https://info.stellic.jhu.edu). Strength: Descriptive. Uses existing data.
8. **Workload, not grades.** If ATLAS adds course-difficulty data, show workload/time expectations and avoid grade distributions. Rests on: Carta grade data lowered GPA; workload data did not — [Stanford News](https://news.stanford.edu/2018/05/10/knowing-past-course-data-affects-college-students-gpa/). Strength: Moderate.
9. **"Bring this to your advisor" export.** A printable/shareable-by-URL plan summary that includes the student's notes, questions, and open decisions, designed for a human conversation (no account, no server). Rests on: counselor-supported interventions outperform computer-only ones — [Sage](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/careerdevelopment/chpt/career-intervention-outcomes); Stellic's advisor-review loop — [Case Western](https://case.edu/registrar/registration-classes/degree-planning). Strength: Strong-indirect.
10. **Progressive disclosure in the college finder.** Start new applicants with a few broad college/meta-major groupings and reveal full course lists on demand. Rests on: novices benefit from fewer choices — [Hechinger](https://hechingerreport.org/some-colleges-start-to-confront-a-surprising-reason-students-fail-too-many-choices/); meta-major grouping practice — [CCRC](https://ccrc.tc.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/guided-pathways-part-1-theory-practice.pdf). Caveat: categorization alone did not reduce overload in one study — [IDEALS](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/49405). Strength: Weak-Mixed.
11. **"Plan by end of first term" milestone.** A gentle on-page prompt (no notifications) for first-years to save one full multi-year plan, even if tentative. Rests on: early program entry and full-program plans linked to completion — [CCRC](https://ccrc.tc.columbia.edu/publications/get-with-the-program.html); [CCRC ACIP](https://ccrc.tc.columbia.edu/publications/ask-connect-inspire-plan-onboarding.html). Strength: Moderate (observational). Caution: Minerva students declare majors later by design.
12. **Interest profiler as a starting point, not a verdict.** If ATLAS's interest-based finder uses RIASEC-like scoring, show the top two or three areas and say results vary across instruments; link to My Next Move rather than re-implementing it. Rests on: different inventories give different top codes — [Illinois Experts](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/what-do-interest-inventories-measure-the-convergence-and-content-/); O*NET IP is free and validated — [O*NET IP Manual](https://onetcenter.org/reports/IP_Manual.html). Strength: Moderate.

### Inferences
- Ideas 1, 2, 4 and 5 have the best evidence-to-effort ratio and all fit "the user does the thinking."
- Avoid: a "recommended for you" major/course list (weak evidence, passivity, likely AI or account), reminder nudges (strong null), grade distributions (Carta harm), and an AI advisor chatbot (forbidden; evidence only supports logistics bots).
- Every idea above stores only in the browser and uses human-written or official data, consistent with ATLAS constraints; idea 9's URL share should encode plan state client-side.

### Gaps
- None of these features has been tested in a Minerva context; effects from US community colleges, NYU, Stanford, and French high schools may not transfer to Minerva's small, global, late-declaring cohort.
- Evidence on the *format* (web tool vs class assignment) is thin: the utility-value and role-model effects come from in-class delivery, and computer-only delivery is weaker per meta-analyses, so ATLAS versions may produce smaller effects.
- Did not check whether ATLAS's outcome statistics already include guess-first or interpretive framing; the report writer should confirm against the app.
