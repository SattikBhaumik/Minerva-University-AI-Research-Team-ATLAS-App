# Applicant needs: exploring majors and deciding whether to apply to Minerva University (and small, unconventional, international universities)

Research date: 2026-11-22. Method note for the report writer: minerva.edu pages were fetched in full with curl on this date and quoted from the page text. Every non-Minerva host this session tried (niche.com, medium.com) is blocked from fetching, so all Reddit, Niche, CollegeVine, Appily, Cappex, College Confidential, Quora and research-organization findings below come from **search-engine snippets or search summaries only** and are marked "(snippet only)". No Reddit or Quora thread showed up in any search result, so no Reddit or Quora thread is quoted directly. Treat snippet-only items as directional. Sources from before 2022 are marked "(older)".

## 1. What questions do Minerva applicants and admitted students most often ask, grouped by theme?

### Takeaway
Minerva's own FAQ (about 100 questions) and its "applicant fears" blog post show what applicants ask most. Outside the admissions process itself, the questions cluster around: the real full cost and how aid works (need-aware, no full rides, no US federal aid); what "online seminars" mean for a "residential" program; which cities students actually live in, and what living there is like (no meal plan, shared rooms, cooking, flights, visas); how and when a major is chosen (it is declared in the second semester of year one, with only five majors); and what happens after graduation. Independent reviews (snippet only) add that the workload is heavy, aid is less reliable than it looks, rotation cities are expensive, and the school is "not for everyone."

### Cited Findings

**Academics and majors**
- Minerva has five undergraduate majors: Arts and Humanities, Business, Computational Sciences, Natural Sciences, Social Sciences. "You will declare your major in the second semester of your first year," after first-year "Cornerstone courses." "College Advisors and Professional Development Coaches will guide you in selecting the major." — [Minerva Majors & Concentrations](https://www.minerva.edu/undergraduate/majors-concentrations/) (fetched 2026-11-22)
- The academic pathway is the same for every major: major-specific core courses start in year 2, students "select a concentration" in year 3 and begin capstone work, and year 4 adds electives, senior tutorials and the finished capstone. Business majors also do a required summer practicum (B199). — [Minerva Majors & Concentrations](https://www.minerva.edu/undergraduate/majors-concentrations/)
- FAQ question: "I can't find the major I'm most interested in studying… Can I create my own major?" Answer: "Individualized majors are not possible, but the combination of concentrations and the ability to do an approved special concentration provides flexibility." — [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/) (fetched 2026-11-22)
- FAQ question: "Can I do a double major or double concentration?" Answer: "Yes." The blog's "Quick Facts" student tables show many double majors (e.g., "Business & Computational Sciences", "Social Sciences & Business"). — [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/); [Minerva blog, 4 Things…](https://www.minerva.edu/blog/4-things-you-shouldnt-worry-about-when-applying-to-minerva-university) (Feb 21, 2024)
- Other recurring academic FAQs: AP credit is not accepted ("we do not accept AP exams for credit at this time"); no foreign-language courses or majors; arts are supported mainly through co-curricular activities and civic partners; "How will lab work be conducted without a permanent campus?" (answer: seminar-based experiment design plus internships and summer labs); advisors are assigned to every student; up to 32 transfer credits. — [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/)
- A first-year student writes that "Programming… was (and sometimes still is) a massive struggle of mine" and relied on "programming summer resources" and "weekly Structured Study Sessions," because "Minerva has programming in the curriculum." This shows that non-STEM applicants may need to know about required quantitative and coding work. — [Minerva blog, Ani Soporean, Class of 2027](https://www.minerva.edu/blog/three-things-i-learned-during-my-first-month-at-minerva) (c. 2023)

**Four-year city rotation**
- FAQ: "Past and current rotation cities include Berlin, Buenos Aires, Hyderabad, San Francisco, Seoul, Taipei, and Tokyo." A separate FAQ answer says current cities are "San Francisco, where students spend their first academic year… followed by other cities such as Berlin, Buenos Aires, and Tokyo." — [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/)
- Applicants ask "How does Minerva determine future residence hall locations?" The answer cites the "quality of the holistic student experience, access to professional opportunities, and availability of secure housing." — [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/)
- The number of cities is described inconsistently over time: "move to six different cities" (transfer student letter, Class of 2020, older); "up to seven different cities" (American Academy of Arts & Sciences, snippet only, older); "5+ diverse cities" (Minerva student-experience page per search summary, snippet only). — [Minerva blog, Connor Mitchell](https://www.minerva.edu/blog/an-open-letter-to-potential-transfer-students-should-you-take-the-leap) (older); [AMACAD](https://www.amacad.org/publication/minerva-intentional-university) (snippet only); [Minerva Student Experience](https://www.minerva.edu/student-experience)

**Online seminar format**
- FAQ: "Do all classes take place online?" Answer: "Minerva's undergraduate program is first and foremost a residential program. All academic classes are synchronous and take place live in real-time via our learning platform. All of Minerva's classes are seminars of 23 students or fewer." — [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/)
- FAQ: "Will students be sitting together around a table and watching a video of the professor or will students be in their dorm rooms?" Answer: there is "no specific required physical location… dorm room, student lounge, local cafe." Office hours are held on the platform, and "multiple faculty members travel to the various rotation cities." — [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/)
- The class-size cap is stated differently by different sources: "23 students or fewer" / "fewer than 23" ([Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/); [Majors page](https://www.minerva.edu/undergraduate/majors-concentrations/): "100% of students have class sizes under 23 students") vs. "capped at 19 students" (Wikipedia, snippet only, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minerva_University)) and "the other 19 students in class" ([Connor Mitchell, older](https://www.minerva.edu/blog/an-open-letter-to-potential-transfer-students-should-you-take-the-leap)). The cap appears to have risen over time, so third-party pages lag.

**Workload and rigor**
- "Managing Minerva, indeed, is an intense and challenging journey… the amount of time spent socializing and in front of my computer felt overwhelming at times." — [Minerva blog, Ani Soporean, Class of 2027](https://www.minerva.edu/blog/three-things-i-learned-during-my-first-month-at-minerva) (c. 2023)
- "I remember telling my academic advisor that I felt as though readings and other schoolwork were never-ending." — [Minerva blog, Alexandru Huțu, Class of 2020](https://www.minerva.edu/blog/how-to-have-a-meaningful-university-experience-without-burning-out) (older)
- "With a flipped classroom approach, engagement is not just rewarded, it's required. At any time during class, I might be called on to defend a position." — [Connor Mitchell, Class of 2020](https://www.minerva.edu/blog/an-open-letter-to-potential-transfer-students-should-you-take-the-leap) (older)
- Niche's summary of student reviews: "fast-paced, rigorous workload combined with frequent international moves demands resilience and adaptability"; "limited options in certain areas." (snippet only; Niche labels this an AI-generated summary) — [Niche](https://www.niche.com/colleges/minerva-university/reviews/)
- A Cappex student review calls the experience "rich but also very challenging" (snippet only). — [Cappex](https://www.cappex.com/colleges/minerva-university-0/reviews)

**Outcomes and jobs**
- Per-major outcome figures on Minerva's majors page: Business 94.8% ("employed… or completing internships"), 97 graduates, 2019–2025; Computational Sciences 95.6%, 256 graduates, 2019–2025; Natural Sciences 85% (43.5% advanced degree, 41.3% employed full-time), 46 graduates, 2019–2025; Social Sciences 93%, 105 sole-major graduates, 2019–2025; Arts and Humanities 82% employed plus 18% advanced degrees, 104 graduates, **2019–2023**. — [Minerva Majors & Concentrations](https://www.minerva.edu/undergraduate/majors-concentrations/)
- FAQ: "Does Minerva support students in arranging internships or is it up to students?" Answer: "Students are responsible for arranging their own internships, supported by Minerva's Coaching and Talent Development team." — [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/)
- The career page says coaching is "integrated throughout" and "continues beyond graduation," with work-study roles, labs, and civic-partner projects. — [Minerva Career Development](https://www.minerva.edu/undergraduate/career-development/)

**Cost and financial aid**
- Estimated cost of attendance 2027/2028: Tuition and Program Fee $31,300; Residential Services Fee $19,900; Student Services Fee $2,800; Estimated Living Costs $5,600 ("not paid to Minerva"); Total $59,600. Extra year-one items: visa and immigration fees "approximately $740"; laptop $800–$2,000; health insurance $700–$800 per semester; $1,000 security deposit; $500 enrollment deposit; "US Tax on Non-Qualified Scholarship Income $0–$700 per semester"; emergency savings "~$1,000 recommended." Years 2–4: "Flights between cities 3+ per year, variable"; "Visas for rotation cities typically twice per year." — [Minerva Tuition & Financial Aid](https://www.minerva.edu/undergraduate/tuition-fees/) (fetched 2026-11-22)
- Aid rules applicants must understand: "need-aware"; "Minerva does not offer full-ride financial aid packages"; packages start "with a loan," then work-study (up to about $5,000 a year), then scholarship; "Minerva does not participate in U.S. Title IV federal financial aid programs"; a CSS Profile is required (school code 6033, no fee waivers); parent bank statements for the last 12 months are required; "Students cannot apply for financial aid after admissions decisions have been released. There are no extensions or exceptions"; aid deadlines fall one week after each admissions deadline (EA Nov 1 / aid Nov 8; RD I Jan 5 / Jan 12; RD II Mar 2 / Mar 9). — [Minerva Tuition & Financial Aid](https://www.minerva.edu/undergraduate/tuition-fees/); [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/)
- Recurring FAQ questions: "Does Minerva cover the costs of flights…?" (No.) "Do I need to supply my parents' financial information… if I am a self-supporting student?" (Yes, with very few exceptions.) "Do I have to provide information for my non-custodial parent?" (Yes.) "Are technology costs, including a computer, included?" (No; students supply a laptop and headset.) — [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/)
- Niche reviewer: "Financially, you should really talk to current students before signing up, as student loans have been increasing every year and financial aid is not always as reliable as it seems." (snippet only; date not shown) — [Niche](https://www.niche.com/colleges/minerva-university/reviews/)
- A student's Medium post argues that moving is expensive in its own right and that "almost half of the rotation cities are among the most expensive globally, like Seoul, San Francisco, and Berlin." (snippet only; date not shown) — [Medium, "Minerva Madness"](https://medium.com/@alhassan_69618/minerva-madness-juggling-life-learning-and-a-suitcase-b6347f5e3b8b)
- Data USA reports 2024 undergraduate tuition of $21,630, up 15.7% from 2023, and average net price up 19.5% between 2023 and 2024 (snippet only). This differs from Minerva's own fee breakdown (tuition and residential fees are split differently, and the year differs), so third-party price pages are a likely source of confusion. — [Data USA](https://datausa.io/profile/university/minerva-university)

**Admissions-process questions (context only; out of scope for coaching)**

- CollegeVine: Minerva should be treated as a reach for anyone "because there is little public data," and its acceptance rate is low for reasons that aren't clear (snippet only). — [CollegeVine](https://collegevine.com/faq/146972/admissions-insights-for-minerva-university)

### Inferences
- Most decision-relevant facts already exist on minerva.edu, but they are scattered across the FAQ, the tuition page, the majors page and the blogs, and some are stated inconsistently (class size 19 vs. 23; 4 vs. 6 vs. 7 cities; "current" vs. "past" cities). The applicant's problem is assembling and reconciling the facts rather than missing them.
- The FAQ itself shows what worries applicants: many answers start with "No" (no meal plan, no sports, no AP credit, no language courses, no individualized majors, no full rides, no Title IV, no extensions). An applicant needs a clear "what Minerva does not have" view to judge fit honestly.
- Major choice at Minerva is a two-stage decision (major at the end of year one, concentration in year three), so "I don't know what to study yet" is an unusually good fit for this model. Applicants still need to see the actual concentrations and courses to know whether their interest is covered.

### Gaps
- No Reddit r/MinervaUniversity or r/ApplyingToCollege thread, Quora answer, or YouTube Q&A appeared in any search result, and those hosts could not be fetched. Question frequencies from those communities could not be measured, so treat the themes above as being drawn from Minerva's FAQ and blog plus review-site snippets.
- The pages fetched have no per-city visa guidance (which visa type, processing time, nationalities with difficulty).
- Dates of individual Niche, Appily and Cappex reviews could not be seen in snippets.

## 2. What misconceptions or worries about Minerva recur?

### Takeaway
The recurring worries are: legitimacy and accreditation, made sharper because accreditation dates only from 2021 and a WSCUC special visit took place in winter 2026; "it's an online school"; no campus, sports or meal plan; whether the experience is worth the cost when aid is loan-first; a low and opaque acceptance rate; and whether rotation and workload are sustainable. Some misconceptions come from outdated third-party pages (the old 19-student cap, "Minerva Schools at KGI," the wrong accreditor).

### Cited Findings
- Accreditation: WSCUC granted initial accreditation in June 2021. "Following a special site visit during the winter of 2026, WSCUC affirmed Minerva University's accreditation. The official action letter was published in March 2026." The page links the "Special Visit Team Report (December 2025)" and the "WSCUC Action Letter (February 2026)." The institution renamed itself Minerva University in 2023. — [Minerva Accreditation](https://www.minerva.edu/accreditation/) (fetched 2026-11-22)
- FAQ: "Is Minerva accredited?" Answer: "regionally accredited by… WSCUC… a diploma with a Bachelor of Science (B.S.) or a Bachelor of Arts (B.A.) that is recognized globally." FAQ: "Will my credits transfer if I decide to leave Minerva?" Answer: "the decision is up to the transfer institution." — [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/)
- CollegeVine notes that Minerva "only received accreditation in 2021" (snippet only). — [CollegeVine](https://www.collegevine.com/questions/21006/should-i-apply-to-minerva)
- One third-party site (CLRN) claims Minerva holds DEAC (distance-education) accreditation, which conflicts with WSCUC's own records. This is misinformation that applicants may meet (snippet only). — [CLRN](https://www.clrn.org/?p=60711); contradicted by [WSCUC institution page](https://wscuc.org/institutions/minerva-university)
- Inside Higher Ed (2021) asked whether accreditation actually helped Minerva (older; snippet only). — [Inside Higher Ed](https://insidehighered.com/node/52318)
- "Online": Wikipedia describes Minerva as "a private online university" (snippet only), while Minerva insists it is "first and foremost a residential program." Minerva's student-experience page draws the line explicitly: "Only the undergraduate program is fully residential… Graduate and executive programs are primarily online." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minerva_University) (snippet only); [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/); [Minerva Student Experience](https://www.minerva.edu/student-experience)
- No campus: a founding-class student, asked whether she missed the traditional experience, answered "the simple answer is, yes" (The PIE News, older, snippet only). — [The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/zoey-haar-founding-class-minerva-schools-at-kgi/)
- Cost and aid reliability: see the Niche quote in Q1 ("financial aid is not always as reliable as it seems") (snippet only). A transfer student wrote: "Did I take out $15,000 in student loans for nothing?" - Acceptance rate: Minerva lists "Minerva's acceptance rate is so low" among its top four applicant fears (2024). — [Minerva blog](https://www.minerva.edu/blog/4-things-you-shouldnt-worry-about-when-applying-to-minerva-university)
- Transfer-credit rules conflict: CollegeVine says transfer students "must enroll as a first-year student" (snippet only, undated), while Minerva's current FAQ says transfers can bring up to 32 credits and "graduate in three years." — [CollegeVine](https://www.collegevine.com/questions/21006/should-i-apply-to-minerva); contradicted by [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/)
- Choosing a major later: Minerva presents this as a feature (declaration in the second semester of year one). The FAQ's "Can I create my own major?" question shows that applicants worry the five majors are too narrow. — [Minerva Majors](https://www.minerva.edu/undergraduate/majors-concentrations/); [Minerva FAQs](https://www.minerva.edu/faqs/)

It gives no thread links, so this is **unverified and low reliability** (snippet only). — [ftp.bills.com.au aggregator](https://ftp.bills.com.au/lunar-tips/minerva-university-honest-reviews-and-reddit-discussions-1767648896)

### Inferences
- Many misconceptions come from stale third-party information (KGI-era naming, a 19-student cap, an old transfer policy, the wrong accreditor). A tool that shows dated facts with "as of" stamps and links to the primary page would directly counter this.
- The 2026 special visit is a fact applicants may find and worry about. Linking the primary WSCUC documents is more honest than summarizing them.

### Gaps
- I did not read the WSCUC Special Visit Team Report (December 2025) or the February 2026 Action Letter, so I cannot say what prompted the special visit. A tool should link those documents rather than characterize them until someone reads them.
- I found no reliable data on how common each worry is.

## 3. What does general research say about how 16–19 year olds choose a major or college, and what information they lack?

### Takeaway
Major choice is the education decision adults most often regret, money is the leading factor in college choice, and first-generation and international students are hit hardest by price opacity (sticker vs. net price), late starts, and visa and cost-of-living uncertainty. All third-party research below is snippet only, and part of it is older (2017) or industry marketing research.

### Cited Findings
- Gallup–Strada (about 90,000 US adults, June 2016–March 2017; older): 51% of those who pursued postsecondary education would change at least one of degree type, institution or major. Field of study was the most common regret (36%), then institution (28%). Bachelor's holders are more likely than other degree holders to regret their field. Gallup links the regret to difficulty "using their education to obtain their preferred job" (snippet only). — [Gallup, "Do You Regret Your College Choices?"](https://news.gallup.com/opinion/gallup/211070/regret-college-choices.aspx); [Strada](https://cci.stradaeducation.org/value/do-you-regret-your-college-choices/)
- EAB: financial concerns remain the top factor in college choice (2022 blog; similar June 2024 post) (snippet only). — [EAB, Gen Z's college search behavior](https://eab.com/insights/blogs/enrollment/gen-zs-college-search-behavior)
- EAB 2024 Mental Health Survey: 45% of students report moderate to extreme mental-health impacts on their college search (snippet only). — [EAB Anxious Generation insight paper](https://pages.eab.com/rs/732-GKV-655/images/EMS-E360-Insight-Paper-Anxious-Generation-PDF.pdf?version=0)
- EAB 2026: about 10% of first-year students had already changed their planned major because of concerns about AI's impact on their path, and a separate EAB AI survey put "considered changing at any point in the search" at 42% (snippet only). — [EAB press: AI impact on careers clouds college decisions](https://eab.com/about/newsroom/press/ai-impact-careers-clouds-students-college-decisions/); [EAB AI Survey Insight Paper (2026)](https://attachment.eab.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/39949-Enroll360-AI-Survey-Insight-Paper-1.pdf)
- EAB (2025): nearly 30% of Gen Z students chose a college at least partly for political reasons (survey data from 2024 and early 2025; snippet only). — [EAB press](https://eab.com/about/newsroom/press/politics-shaping-gen-z-college-choices/)
- Ruffalo Noel Levitz (2024, 11th–12th graders): 69% of first-generation vs. 64% of continuing-generation students ruled out schools based on sticker price. The authors suggest first-gen students may be less familiar with the sticker vs. net price distinction (industry research; snippet only). — [RNL, Beyond Sticker Shock](https://www.ruffalonl.com/blog/enrollment/beyond-sticker-shock-helping-families-see-the-true-cost-of-college/)
- Niche (survey of 41,911 students, for the 2023 cycle): first-generation students started their search later and rated virtual tours and events, and net price calculators, as more valuable (industry research; snippet only). — [Niche Enrollment Insights](https://www.niche.com/about/enrollment-insights/recruiting-first-generation-college-students-for-2023/)
- Policy brief (2025): net price calculators are hard to find on college sites and often require tax information families do not have at hand (snippet only). — [Economic Strategy Group, Levine](https://www.economicstrategygroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Levine-College-Pricing.pdf)
- Peer-reviewed (Springer, 2026): many prospective students "encounter a complex pricing system that requires them to make a series of high-stakes decisions with limited information"; by 2022–23 only 10% of new students paid the published price (snippet only). — [Innovative Higher Education, Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10755-026-09914-4)
- NBER working paper (2025, Arizona State University data): first-generation students "enter college less informed and with more uncertain beliefs," and a targeted first-year program reduced these knowledge frictions (snippet only). — [NBER w34129](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34129)
- QS International Student Survey: cost of living is the top worry (67% across destinations in a later QS report; over 70% for Australia-bound students in 2024). Half of prospective US-bound students see the US as expensive, and an equal share associate it with "a complex visa process." Post-study work-visa access matters more than before, and "55% of students prioritise affordability" (snippet only). — [QS, What will make international students choose the US](https://www.qs.com/insights/what-will-make-international-students-choose-the-us); [QS ISS 2024 US & Canada report](https://insights.qs.com/hubfs/Reports/Destination%20US%20and%20Canada%20-%20QS%20International%20Student%20Survey%202024%20Report.pdf); [QS ISS](https://www.qs.com/international-student-survey)
- Minerva's own advice to applicants (2016, older) urges asking about class sizes, who teaches, off-campus engagement, diversity and fit, and to "talk to current students." — [Minerva blog, 5 Questions to Ask](https://www.minerva.edu/blog/5-questions-to-ask-before-deciding-on-a-university)

### Inferences
- The general research and Minerva-specific evidence point to the same gaps: true net cost, what a major leads to, and (for internationals) visa and cost-of-living burden. Minerva compounds the cost gap: it does not use FAFSA or Title IV, it requires the CSS Profile with no waivers, aid is loan-first, and there is a hard rule that aid must be requested before the admissions decision.
- Choice overload is a weaker problem at Minerva (five majors) than at large universities. The harder problem is mapping one's interests onto unfamiliar labels ("Computational Sciences," "Cornerstone," the concentrations).

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous 2022–2026 study specific to applicants to small, unconventional or international universities, and none on choice overload in major selection among 16–19 year olds. The Gallup–Strada data is about adults and from 2017.
- No NACAC or Common App research surfaced in the searches; a targeted follow-up is needed.

## 4. Which needs could be served without collecting user data and without AI-generated text?

### Takeaway
Nearly every evidenced need can be met by presenting Minerva's own published information (with source links and "as of" dates), simple client-side calculators over published numbers, side-by-side comparisons, and human-curated student voices. None of these requires accounts, tracking, or generated prose. The main exceptions are personalized aid estimates (no official net price calculator was found) and live peer Q&A (which needs a human-moderated channel).

### Inferences
- Rows 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 11 are pure presentation of published Minerva facts. Rows 1 and 12 are calculators or worksheets that can run entirely in the browser (any persistence limited to the user's own device), which meets the "no user data" constraint.
- Rows 3 and 12 invite the applicant to compare and reflect rather than receive a recommendation. This fits the project's rule against AI-written copy and its learning-orientation test.
- Row 10 is the only one that depends on a human editor's ongoing curation; its quotes must carry their dates because the program has changed (class size, cities).

### Gaps
- I found no official Minerva net price calculator in the pages fetched, so a personalized aid estimate cannot be offered from public data. The tool can only show published fee lines plus the user's own inputs, and should say so.
- Per-city visa requirements, the current rotation sequence by class year, and course catalogs per concentration were not found in the pages fetched; they may be in the Undergraduate Handbook or the Financing Your Education Guide (linked from minerva.edu but not read in this session).
- Live peer Q&A ("talk to current students," which Niche and Minerva both recommend) cannot be served without a human-moderated channel. Linking Minerva's own events and student videos is the data-free fallback.
