## 2. "Completion and Retention Rates - November 2025": exact rates by cohort

### Takeaway
VERIFIED. The 150% graduation rates by cohort run from 75.00% (founding class) to 90.18% (class of 2023), with a cumulative 83.76% for all cohorts and 85.13% for the last three. First-to-second-year retention runs from 87.07% to 98.25%, with a cumulative 94.62% for all cohorts. Every figure the advocacy mirror reported (defundminerva.org) matches the original.

### Cited Findings
All figures below are VERIFIED and come from [Completion and Retention Rates - November 2025](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/6915f03bace3ce80e1ea0cfe_Completion%20and%20Retention%20Rates%20-%20November%202025.pdf).

- **p. 1, "Undergraduate Retention Rates: First Year to Second Year"** (Class / Retention Rate): M19_FC 92.86%; M19_IC 87.39%; M20 95.63%; M21 96.45%; M22 93.67%; M23 92.02%; M24 87.07%; M25 98.25%; M26 94.02%; M27 95.77%; M28 96.12%.
- **p. 1, cumulative first-to-second retention:** "All Cohorts 94.62%"; "Last 5 Cohorts 95.77%"; "Last 3 Cohorts 95.40%". Footnote: "**Excludes Visiting Scholar Year (VSY) program".
- **p. 1 and p. 2, cohort definitions (verbatim):** "*FC = Founding Class IC = Inaugural Class. MXX = graduation year. FC entered in Fall 2014 and completed the first year of studies; IC entered in Fall 2015 and completed the first year of studies. The two cohorts joined in Fall 2016 to form one graduating class of 2019 (with FC having an expected time to completion of five years including the gap year immediately following their first year)."
- **p. 2, "Undergraduate Completion Rates", Cohort / 150% Graduation Rates:** M19_FC 75.00%; M19_IC 79.28%; M20 80.63%; M21 86.80%; M22 83.54%; M23 90.18%; M24 80.37%; M25 85.03%.
- **p. 2, cumulative 150% graduation rates:** "All Cohorts 83.76%"; "Last 3 Cohorts 85.13%". Visiting Scholar Year is excluded.
- The document gives no cohort sizes (headcounts), no definition of "retention" or "completion" beyond the table headings, and no outcome or placement data.
- p. 3 covers the graduate Master's and Certificate in Decision Analysis only (out of scope).
- Cross-check: defundminerva.org's snippet figures ("Class of 2025 at 85.0% … 98.3% returning", "83.76%", founding class "75%") match M25 85.03%, M25 retention 98.25%, all-cohort 83.76% and M19_FC 75.00%. The mirror was accurate — [defundminerva.org/ask](https://www.defundminerva.org/ask/) (the mirror itself was not opened)

### Inferences
- The two lowest retention cohorts (M19_IC 87.39%, M24 87.07%) line up with the lowest completion cohorts (M19_IC 79.28%, M24 80.37%). M24 entered in fall 2020, the pandemic year; that timing link is my inference.
- Minerva's own completion figures (80-90% per cohort since M20) are a little lower than the 88.8% IPEDS figure quoted by aggregators. The definitions differ: Minerva covers all cohorts, while the IPEDS Outcome Measures are limited to non-Pell first-time, full-time students (see section 5).

### Gaps
- No cohort headcounts, so the cumulative rates cannot be re-derived.

## 3. WSCUC Special Visit Team Report (Dec 2-5, 2025 visit) and Action Letter (Feb 27, 2026): outcomes content

### Takeaway
VERIFIED. Neither document reports graduate placement rates, graduation rates, or any review of how Minerva measures career outcomes. The visit covered five finance and governance areas plus natural-science labs. The only outcome-type numbers appear in the Natural Sciences section (p. 26), and they are Minerva-supplied figures that the team relays. The team says it is "clear that Minerva graduates have been successful thus far". It also asks Minerva to assess its internship infrastructure "in the aggregate", in addition to its "deep and commendable understanding of the success of individual students". The report describes retention as "historically between 95 and 97 percent", which is higher than Minerva's own all-cohort figure of 94.62%.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED, team report p. 1: "REPORT OF THE WSCUC TEAM SPECIAL VISIT To Minerva University December 2-5, 2025". Chair: Eduardo Ochoa (President Emeritus, CSU Monterey Bay). The table of contents on p. 2 lists the sections: Financial Strategy and Transition; Institutional Communication and Organizational Culture; Cost of Attendance; Library Resources; Natural Science Curriculum and Laboratory Experiences. There is no student-outcomes or graduation section. — [Team Report](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/69b426e1703eed3ca118d714_MU%20Special%20Visit%20(SV)%20Team%20Report%20FINAL%20Draft%202026.01.02%20(1).pdf)
- VERIFIED, p. 3: "As of fall 2025, Minerva enrolled 572 undergraduates, all of whom are full time. Approximately 85% are international…" Also: "Minerva does not currently participate in US government Title IV financial aid programs." — Team Report (link above)
- VERIFIED, p. 11: "changes to US visa policies and related circumstances increased the summer 2025 'melt' of new undergraduate students to nearly 20%, from less than 10% historically, with the result that the fall 2025 undergraduate enrollment, including visiting scholars, fell to 572 from a high of 632 in FY25." — Team Report
- VERIFIED, p. 20: "High retention rates (historically between 95 and 97 percent) are expected to support this growth…" — Team Report. This **conflicts** with Minerva's Nov 2025 PDF: all cohorts 94.62%, cohorts ranging 87.07-98.25%, last five cohorts 95.77% — [Completion and Retention Rates](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/6915f03bace3ce80e1ea0cfe_Completion%20and%20Retention%20Rates%20-%20November%202025.pdf)
- VERIFIED, p. 26, verbatim: "Data provided in the institution's SV report indicate that Minerva students have been successfully acquiring laboratory research experiences in support of post-graduate goals. Per the report, 98% of Natural Sciences students whose stated goal was to pursue graduate school obtained research experiences in external research laboratories during the semester and/or summer. More generally, on average, over 50% of the 2019 to 2024 Natural Sciences cohorts undertook a laboratory science research-related internship, with 100% of these students subsequently pursuing an advanced degree or a science-related career." Footnote 11: "Note this period is inclusive of COVID-19 pandemic." — Team Report
- VERIFIED, p. 26: the team met "six Natural Sciences alumni now in PhD programs". — Team Report
- VERIFIED, p. 27, verbatim: "the team encourages Minerva to develop a robust plan to systematically assess the effectiveness of its infrastructure and, in doing so, develop a better understanding of how well the internship program serves students in the aggregate, thus complementing its deep and commendable understanding of the success of individual students." The report also notes that internship managers "complete a survey designed to 'help Minerva better understand how we prepare students specifically for professional opportunities.'" — Team Report
- VERIFIED, p. 28: "In summary, the team found that Minerva has made good progress in demonstrating the effectiveness of its approach to laboratory science education. It is clear that Minerva graduates have been successful thus far." — Team Report
- VERIFIED, Action Letter p. 1: the Commission acted "at its meeting February 13, 2025" (sic; the visit was December 2025 and the letter is dated February 27, 2026, so this is almost certainly a typo for 2026). Actions: "Receive the Special Visit team report that focused on five required areas: (1) financial model; (2) institutional communication and organizational culture; (3) cost of attendance; (4) library resources; and (5) natural science curriculum and laboratory experiences." It also reschedules reaffirmation "with the Offsite Review in fall 2027 and the Accreditation Visit in spring 2028". — [Action Letter](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64ca995f0fd30a33b2fd01cc/69b426e177b6b85d9e85edf1_CAL_260227_MINERVA_SV.pdf)
- VERIFIED, Action Letter p. 2: the commendations include "Improving transparency and predictability of cost of attendance for students". The two required "Areas for Development" are strengthening the financial position (CFRs 3.4, 3.5) and "formalizing policies, establishing organizational structures, and standardizing processes" (CFRs 1.4, 3.11, 4.8). **The letter contains no reference to graduation rates, student outcomes or career data.** — Action Letter

### Inferences
- The accreditor did not independently review or audit the 91% placement figure. The only outcome evidence the team engaged with was Natural Sciences lab-internship data from Minerva's own Special Visit report, plus six alumni interviews.
- The team's "95 to 97 percent" retention appears to describe recent cohorts loosely. It is not supported by the all-cohort figure.

### Gaps
- Minerva's institutional "SV report" and its exhibits, the source of the 98% and "over 50%" Natural Sciences figures, are not public as far as I found.

### Inferences
- **The SS headline does not reconcile.** By its own definition ("employed full-time or enrolled in advanced degree programs"), the parts give 74% + 20% = 94%, not 93%. Adding the 7% internships gives 101%. At least one figure is wrong or rounded inconsistently. On n = 105, 93% is about 98 people; 74% is about 78, 20% about 21 and 7% about 7, for about 106 people, which is more than the sample. This is my arithmetic.
- **NS 50% vs 43.5%.** "Graduate Education 50%" in the industries chart (about 23 of 46) conflicts with "43.5% are pursuing an advanced degree" (20 of 46). The industries chart may use a different denominator, perhaps only graduates with a known destination (it sums to 89.1%), or it may count graduate-education employment such as research or teaching roles at universities. The brochure does not explain the difference.
- **"Highest" claims conflict across brochures.** CS says "79.1% of CS + Business graduates are employed full-time, the highest rate among all double major combinations". SS says "76% of SS + Business graduates enter full-time employment directly, the highest rate among all double major combinations". The two cannot both be true unless the definitions differ.
- **Headline definitions differ by college,** so the five headlines are not comparable:
  - BZ 94.8% = full-time employment + internships (advanced degrees, 1%, apparently left out, since 75.3 + 18.6 = 93.9, not 94.8).
  - CS 95.6% = employment + internships + advanced degrees.
  - NS 85% and SS 93% = full-time employment + advanced degrees.
  - AH 82% + 18% = 100%, so in effect it reports no one as unemployed or unknown.
  - Without internships, the full-time employment or advanced-degree rates would be about BZ 76.3%, CS 84.5%, NS 84.8%, SS 94% (my arithmetic).
- **Samples are a fraction of graduates.** Each brochure says Minerva "tracked outcomes for more than 300" (BZ, CS, SS) or "more than 130" (NS). The headline samples are 97, 256, 105 and 46. That is under a third of Business and Social Sciences graduates, under half of Natural Sciences graduates, and well under all CS graduates, unless "tracked" includes double majors (stated only for SS). The selection rule (sole majors, respondents to a survey, or something else) is stated only for SS.
- **The windows differ from the institution-wide report.** These brochures cover classes 2019-2025 (AH: 2019-2023) and claim "within six months of graduation". The 2023 Professional Ventures report measured a single November 2022 snapshot for classes 2019-2022. The brochures are therefore a newer, separate dataset, not a breakdown of the 91%.

### Gaps
- No brochure states how the data was collected (survey, LinkedIn, career-office records), the response rate, how the six-month window was enforced for the 2019-2021 classes, or how double majors were assigned. The SS footnote alone says "surveyed" and "sole major".
- There is no institution-wide total that reconciles the five samples: 104 + 97 + 256 + 46 + 105 = 608, with different cohort ranges.
- The minerva.edu majors page was not re-fetched in this pass. The match to it relies on the coordinator's list of majors-page figures, and every one of them appears verbatim in the brochures above.
